Gizmodo navigation redesign

In 2019 I was part of the team of 4 designers rethinking navigation on the Kinja platform, where readers kept getting lost between sites and sections. Through two rounds of prototyping and moderated user testing, we raised task success from 60% to 94.4% and shipped a navigation that measurably improved engagement across the whole network.

Kinja was the publishing platform powering sites like Gizmodo, The Onion, Jezebel, Kotaku, The A.V. Club and more. Together these brands reached around 100 million unique readers a month.

Readers couldn't tell where they were

Kinja had many page types, sections & story formats with independent navigation components. Notice how navigation collapses on the example above: the section menu disappears, the logo changes and now links to the Muse front page. There's no obvious way to get back to the Jezebel front page. This was the main problem.

We came up with 2 additional goal statements, so the final goals were:

  • Increase engagement by allowing readers to explore content across front pages, sections, and article pages with confidence.
  • Expand site brand voices while maintaining simplicity of a consistent navigation UI.
  • Network effect: introduce users to sites across the network through a clear navigation structure.

Three concepts, six prototypes

After sketching there was enough diagreement that we decided to let evidence pick the winner an user-test 3 concepts against each other.

2 factors that made this situation unique:

  • With a 50–50 reader mobile-desktop split, every concept had to be designed and tested twice, once for each form factor.
  • This was the first project at Kinja to rely heavily on qualitative user testing, so we had to build the research foundations — guidelines, screeners, scoring — from scratch.
